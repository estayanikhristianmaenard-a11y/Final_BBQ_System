## Project direction

Keep HTML, CSS, vanilla JavaScript, PHP, MySQL/MariaDB, and XAMPP. Improve the
existing application incrementally. Use ngrok for public HTTPS access during
the professor presentation. The laptop, Apache, MySQL, internet connection, and
tunnel must remain running. Permanent hosting is a later deployment decision.

The customer ordering QR contains the public menu URL. It is separate from any
payment QR generated by a payment provider. Provide the clickable URL beside it.

## Customer workflow

1. Scan the ordering QR or open the link.
2. Browse the public menu: photos, prices, availability, hours, pickup location,
   and whether ordering is open, closed, or temporarily paused.
3. Build a cart before signing in. Preserve the cart through authentication.
4. Verify email using the existing OTP mechanism at checkout; reuse a valid
   signed-in session. Collect a pickup name and optional notes.
5. Review the server-calculated total and choose cash at pickup or online payment.
6. Submit once and receive an order number with a clear next step.
7. Follow the applicable payment path below, then track preparation and pickup.

## Online payment: pay before acceptance

Submit order -> Awaiting payment -> PayMongo test checkout -> Backend verifies
payment -> Awaiting staff acceptance -> Accepted -> Preparing -> Ready for
pickup -> Completed.

- Create checkout sessions in PHP using database prices and quantities. Keep
  secret keys out of browser JavaScript, logs, and Git.
- Reserve stock for a limited payment window. The duration is a configurable
  implementation setting, not an agreed fixed number of minutes.
- Permit staff acceptance and preparation only after verified payment.
- Verify webhook signatures, provider mode, payment amount, currency, and the
  associated order. Handle duplicate events without duplicating stock changes,
  receipts, or notifications.
- A browser success redirect or payment screenshot does not establish payment.
- Expire abandoned checkouts and release reservations safely. Reconcile delayed
  payment events; if payment succeeds after expiry or stock release, flag it for
  manager handling rather than silently accepting an unfulfillable order.
- If a paid order is rejected, record refund-required status and give staff a
  defined manager refund workflow. Do not label money refunded until confirmed.
- Use a same-phone e-wallet checkout where supported; scanning a payment QR is
  not a mandatory step for every online payment.

## Cash payment

Submit order -> Awaiting staff acceptance (unpaid) -> Accepted -> Preparing ->
Ready for pickup -> Staff confirms collected payment -> Completed.

Cash orders remain subject to verification, limits, and staff review. Online
prepayment requirements must not accidentally block the cash acceptance path.

## Payment and preparation are separate

Suggested payment states: UNPAID, PENDING, PAID, FAILED, EXPIRED,
REFUND_REQUIRED, REFUNDED. Final database names will be selected during schema
design. Record method, amount, provider references, and confirmation timestamps.

Preparation states remain NEW/awaiting acceptance, ACCEPTED, PREPARING, READY,
COMPLETED, and CANCELLED, with rejection/expiry outcomes represented explicitly
where needed. Record who changed a state and when. A paid order still requires
staff acceptance. Completion requires pickup and confirmed payment.

Show an order confirmation immediately. Make the final receipt available after
payment and completion, with an emailed copy and retry handling. Preserve old
receipt numbers, price snapshots, and order history during any migration.

## Shop operations and abuse controls

Implemented controls and their current defaults are documented in
[STORE-ORDERING.md](STORE-ORDERING.md): daily hours/manual ordering status,
pickup details, preparation estimates, account/quantity limits, customer
cancellation before acceptance, and owner-only automatic status checks.
No-show recording and review remain a possible future enhancement, outside this
simple pickup implementation.

- Managers set available stock, product prices, store hours, and ordering status.
- Staff review pending orders and see payment status beside preparation status.
- Kitchen staff see actionable accepted orders and move them through preparation.
- Release reservations for rejected, expired, or early-cancelled orders exactly
  once. After preparation starts, staff record reusable stock or waste explicitly.
- Allow customer cancellation before acceptance under rules that account for
  payment already received. After acceptance, require staff handling.
- Start with one active order per account, a configurable submission cooldown,
  and manager-controlled quantity limits. Previously suggested two-minute
  cooldown is a starting proposal to validate, not a mandatory business rule.
- Enforce limits on the server. Use account limits primarily, with IP limits as
  an additional signal because multiple customers may share Wi-Fi.
- Record confirmed no-shows, with manager review before restricting future
  orders. Large orders or repeated no-shows may require verified prepayment.
- Report collected payments separately from pending orders, unpaid amounts,
  refunds, cancellations, and waste. Define reporting dates consistently.

## Presentation payment mode

Current choice: use the built-in [payment simulator](PAYMENT-DEMO.md). Checkout
offers Cash or Demo payment, with success/failure/cancellation scenarios and no
provider account. All demo transactions are marked and excluded from real sales.
The provider-specific notes below apply only to optional future integration.

Use PayMongo test credentials and visibly mark simulated payments and sales.
Test payments do not prove that a real customer will pay or collect food.
Confirm account access and enabled test payment methods before implementation.
PayMongo's current Hosted Checkout quick start lists KYC as a prerequisite.

For QR Ph, follow PayMongo's simulation URL flow. Its testing guide warns that
scanning and paying a generated payment QR can process a real transaction even
in test mode. Use simulated e-wallet/card payments for the presentation.

### Responsive and adaptive interface requirement

Apply responsive layouts across customer, staff, and manager pages. Adapt the
presentation to the available screen width and input method, using the same
website and business rules across phones, tablets, and desktops.

- Use mobile-first CSS, flexible grids, responsive images, and content-driven
  breakpoints. Support portrait and landscape without horizontal page scrolling.
- Make navigation compact on phones, keep primary ordering actions easy to
  reach, and preserve cart and form state when layout changes.
- Use readable text, labeled forms, suitable email/number keyboards, visible
  keyboard focus, sufficient contrast, and comfortably sized touch controls.
- Reflow kitchen columns and manager reports into usable mobile views. Keep
  essential information and actions available; provide contained scrolling for
  genuinely wide tables rather than hiding data.
- Show understandable loading, empty, validation, connection-loss, and retry
  states. Avoid duplicate orders or payments when customers tap repeatedly.
- Verify representative widths from 320px through desktop, tablets, orientation
  changes, and 200% zoom. Check key flows on real Android/iPhone browsers where
  available, plus browser emulation. Record any untested device coverage.

### Delivery order

1. Baseline and backup: run existing workflows against an isolated database,
   confirm defects, and verify backup/restore without replacing live data.
2. Schema and business rules: add payment records, stock reservation/expiry
   handling, store controls, limits, and audit fields with a non-destructive
   migration. Define cancellation, late-payment, and refund behavior.
3. Customer experience: public menu/cart, preserved cart through OTP login,
   pickup name, checkout, clear confirmations, and mobile layouts.
4. Anti-abuse: active-order limits, throttling, quantity limits, duplicate-submit
   protection, staff review, and no-show records.
5. PayMongo test integration: backend session creation, payment selection,
   signature-verified webhooks, payment reconciliation, and failure/retry flows.
6. Staff/kitchen: payment-aware acceptance, kitchen queue, cancellation handling,
   pickup/payment confirmation, and visible notification updates.
7. Manager tools: catalog, stock/reservations/waste, store controls, personnel,
   refunds, reports, and receipt email visibility.
8. Public demo access: expose only the application through ngrok; protect
   configuration, SQL snapshots, logs, tools, and database administration. Set
   production-style error handling and appropriate HTTPS session settings.
9. QR and presentation: generate a downloadable ordering QR for the selected
   public URL and test it from another phone using mobile data.
10. End-to-end validation: verify the complete customer/staff/manager demo,
    prepare sample data and a presentation checklist, and document startup steps.

## Required validation

Public startup, manager QR download/print, and local TEST-webhook setup are now
implemented. See [demo setup](DEMO-SETUP.md) and the
[presentation checklist](DEMO-CHECKLIST.md). Real sender delivery, provider TEST
checkout/webhook delivery, and physical-phone scanning still require manual verification.

- Public browsing and cart survive email verification.
- Customers cannot access staff or manager actions or other customers' orders.
- Concurrent orders cannot oversell the last available items.
- Duplicate submission, webhook retries, and expiry cannot double-deduct or
  double-release stock.
- Unpaid online orders cannot be accepted or prepared.
- Cash orders can be accepted unpaid but cannot complete without payment.
- Failed, abandoned, delayed, and duplicate payment events have clear outcomes.
- Rejection of paid orders requires a traceable refund outcome.
- Receipts and collected-sales reports reconcile to payments and completed orders.
- Real phones can use the QR, checkout, status page, and pickup confirmation.
- Customer, staff, and manager layouts remain usable on phone, tablet, and
  desktop widths, with touch and keyboard navigation and no clipped actions.
- A fresh install, backup restoration, and demo restart follow documented steps.

### Pickup or delivery (current)

At checkout customers choose free pickup or delivery with a fixed ₱20 fee.
Delivery requires a complete address and contact number. Food subtotal and fee
are shown separately; order, receipt, and payment totals include the fee.
Cash can be paid at pickup or on delivery; demo payments happen before acceptance.
Staff see the order type, address, contact, and fee in Kitchen and Order history.
The existing New → Accepted → Preparing → Ready → Completed flow stays the same.
For delivery, Ready means food is ready for dispatch; staff coordinate delivery
manually and complete the order after the food is delivered and payment confirmed.
Preparation estimates do not include travel time. No maps or live GPS tracking.
Earlier pickup-only scope notes are superseded by this addition.
